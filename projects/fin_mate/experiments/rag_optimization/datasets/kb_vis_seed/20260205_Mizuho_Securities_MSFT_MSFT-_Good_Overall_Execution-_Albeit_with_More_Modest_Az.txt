Good Overall Execution, Albeit with More Modest Azure Upside
MSFT reported a good overall F2Q, as total revenue of $81.3B (+15% Y/Y CC) was above
the Street's $80.3B estimate. Azure revenue grew by 38% Y/Y CC and exceeded mgmt's
guidance of ~37% Y/Y CC, but it came in slightly below buy-side expectations. Mgmt
did note that its disciplined capacity allocation weighed on Azure growth. Meanwhile,
Commercial bookings exceeded expectations, growing by a tremendous 228% Y/Y
(largely from OpenAI). Moreover, mgmt is targeting Azure revenue growth of 37-38% Y/
Y CC in F3Q, above the Street's ~36-37% projection, while MSFT's revenue guidance was
in line with consensus. MSFT also expects capex to be down sequentially.
More broadly, we remain ever-confident that MSFT's revenue growth opportunities over
the medium-term and beyond are greater than many realize, and we remain very bullish
on its tangible AI adoption and monetization levers. We reiterate our Outperform rating
and $620 PT.
Key Points
The Numbers
Total revenue of $81.3B (+17% Y/Y reported and 15% on a CC basis) was nicely above our
$80.2B forecast and the Street's similar $80.3B estimate (+15% Y/Y). This also equated
to a roughly 1.2% revenue beat, although below the average 2.4% beat over the prior
4 quarters. By segment, Intelligent Cloud (incl. Azure) and PBP exceeded expectations,
although MPC was surprisingly only roughly in line. Much more detail below.
Good Azure Performance, But with Allocations Constraining Growth Somewhat
Azure revenue growth of 38% Y/Y CC exceeded mgmt's guidance that had called for
37% growth, although this did fall shy of buy-side expectations (and slightly below the
39% growth in F1Q). On the heels of a strong first quarter, F2Q Commercial bookings
astoundingly grew 228% Y/Y CC, with the vast majority of the growth driven by Azure
commitments from both OpenAI and Anthropic. Mgmt did also note that having to
allocate additional capacity to certain first-party applications (such as M365 Copilot), as
well as to R&D, weighed on Azure growth this quarter by at least a couple of points.
Encouraging Azure Growth Outlook, with F3Q Revenue in Line
MSFT provided F3Q revenue guidance of $80.6B-$81.75B (15%-17% Y/Y) that was in line
with the Street's $81.2B target. More importantly, mgmt is targeting Azure revenue growth
of 37-38% Y/Y CC, slightly above the Street's roughly 36-37% Y/Y projection. Meanwhile,
mgmt stated that it expects capex to be down Q/Q. More on guidance below.
Valuation
Using an AH price of $452 (-6%), MSFT trades at a CY26E-27E P/E multiple of 26x and
21x-22x, and a temporarily elevated CY27E EV/FCF multiple of ~31x during this higher
capital intensity period. Our $620 PT (unchanged) is largely based on our updated sum-of-
the-parts model. Our price target also reflects a CY26E-27E P/E multiple of ~35x and 29x.

Base Case/PT
MSFT continues to show it can execute at a generally high level, and that it is a clear
beneficiary of GenAI adoption and monetization. We also expect MSFT to continue to
demonstrate a commitment to profitable growth. Our $620 PT is largely based on our
SoTP model. Our PT also reflects a CY26-27E P/E multiple of 35x and 29x.
Bull Case/PT
Core Azure growth accelerates further, and MSFT’s GenAI contribution becomes stronger
and more diversified via faster adoption of Microsoft 365 Copilot, GitHub Copilot, and
Security Copilot. Impressive operating margin expansion persists even as the company
invests in key growth areas (GenAI, Fabric, etc.). Our bull case valuation utilizes a
CY26-27E P/E multiple of 40x and 33x, equating to $700.
Bear Case/PT
Core Azure growth decelerates more than expected due to increasing competition, an
execution misstep and/or a worsening macro environment. Adoption of GenAI offerings
shows signs of plateauing after the initial adoption wave. Operating margins are lower
than expected, as MSFT cannot remotely offset investments that increasingly impact the
model. Our bear case valuation utilizes a CY26-27E P/E multiple of 24x and 20x, equating
to $420.
Risks
Core consumption growth decelerates more than anticipated as macro challenges
resurface. Competition among cloud infrastructure providers materially increases, leading
to pricing pressure. GenAI adoption and monetization is slower than expected. Azure
suffers a significant breach, triggering churn and a loss of customer confidence. MSFT
fails to anticipate and adapt to evolving technology trends. Global PC shipments slow and
the “AI PC” era does not in fact help MSFT. Ongoing success and dominance across a
range of enterprise markets globally prompts increased regulatory scrutiny. Satya Nadella
leaves the company (we also view Amy Hood and Scott Guthrie as particularly key
employees).

A Closer Look at the Quarter
Total revenue of $81.3B (+17% Y/Y reported and 15% on a CC basis) was nicely above our $80.2B
forecast and the Street's similar $80.3B estimate (+15% Y/Y). Further, and more importantly, Azure
revenue growth of 38% Y/Y CC, which exceeded mgmt's guidance that had called for 37% growth
Y/Y, although this did fall just shy of buy-side expectations. For more specifics on the Azure
performance this quarter, please see below.
On a segment basis, Productivity and Business Processes (PBP) revenue of ~$34.1B (+14% Y/
Y CC) was above our $33.46B projection. Meanwhile, Intelligent Cloud revenue of $32.9B (+28%
Y/Y CC vs. 27% growth reported previously) also exceeded our $32.45B estimate this quarter.
Finally, More Personal Computing (MPC) revenue of nearly $14.3B (-3% Y/Y CC) edged our $14.26B
forecast.
On the heels of a very strong first quarter, F2Q Commercial bookings astoundingly grew 228%
Y/Y CC, which reflects significant further acceleration from the already massive 111% growth
rate reported previously and ~30% Y/Y growth reported in F4Q. The strength this quarter was
predominantly driven by Azure commitments from OpenAI, as well as Anthropic, and with healthy
growth across its core annuity sales motions. More specifically, this includes the incremental
$250B in Azure commitments from the recent OpenAI agreement.
Despite continued high capex, reported gross margins were very good at 68.0%, easily above our
67.0% target and the Street's ~67.1% forecast. Further, OMs of 47.1% were much better than
expected and easily surpassed our and the Street's 45.3% target. In addition, under the equity
method of accounting, OpenAI contributed a $9.978B gain (vs. a $4.061B loss previously) to Other
Income & Expense (OI&E), and a $7.583B net gain (vs. $3.086B net loss previously). Non-GAAP
EPS - excluding this component - was $4.14 vs. our $3.96 estimate and the Street's $3.91 forecast.
Finally, CFFO of $35.8B came in well above the Street's $30.4B estimate.
Good Azure Performance, But With Allocations Constraining Growth Somewhat
As noted above, Azure revenue growth of 38% Y/Y CC, which exceeded mgmt's guidance that
had called for 37% growth Y/Y, although this did come in slightly below buy-side expectations.
This was also slightly below the 39% growth rate reported in F1Q. Having said that, mgmt did also
indicate that having to allocate additional GPU capacity to certain first-party applications (such
as Microsoft 365 Copilot and GitHub Copilot), as well as to R&D and further product innovation,
weighed on Azure growth this quarter by at least a couple of points.
Encouraging Azure Growth Outlook, with F3Q Revenue in Line with Consensus
Regarding guidance, MSFT provided F3Q revenue guidance of $80.6B-$81.75B (15%-17% Y/Y),
which was in line with the Street's $81.2B target (but below our more aggressive ~$82B estimate).
More importantly, we had believed MSFT would guide F3Q Azure growth at least in line, and
to that end mgmt is targeting Azure revenue growth of 37-38% Y/Y CC, above the Street's
roughly 36-37% Y/Y projection. Mgmt also rightly pointed out that the prior year compare
included significantly accelerating growth rates in F3Q25 (and F4Q25). Further on this topic, we
are assuming that MSFT will remain capacity-constrained through the end of this fiscal year,
even as it races to add a lot more (including a nearly stunning 1GW this quarter). Mgmt also
expects FX to serve as a 3-point benefit to total revenue growth in F3Q, and a <1 point tailwind to
F4Q revenue growth.
Meanwhile, MSFT's F3Q COGS guidance of $26.65B-$26.85B came in slightly above the Street's
~$26.6B estimate, but below our higher $26.9B forecast. Additionally, MSFT's opex guidance of
$17.8B-$17.9B was also slightly above the Street's ~$17.8B estimate (Mizuho: ~$18B). And as
such, mgmt expects operating margins to be down slightly Y/Y. Relatedly, mgmt also reiterated
its other income outlook will no longer include any impact from its OpenAI investments that are
remarkably difficult to forecast.
As it relates to capex, mgmt expects it will be down Q/Q, which we note would be for the first
time in four quarters. Still, as a reminder, mgmt previously noted on its last earnings call that it
expects its capex growth rate to be higher in FY26E vs. FY25. Mgmt also again did not explicitly
address its prior FY26 expectations of double-digit revenue growth and operating income, although
it does now expect operating margins to grow slightly Y/Y (vs. it being relatively unchanged Y/

Other Key Points / What Else Piqued Our Interest
- Microsoft Cloud revenue of $51.5B grew 24% Y/Y CC, down slightly from 25% growth last quarter.
Microsoft Cloud gross margin was 67%, slightly better than mgmt's internal expectations and
down roughly 3 points Y/Y (also down 100bps sequentially) as this was impacted by continued
investments in AI infrastructure and growing AI product usage.
- As aforementioned, F2Q Commercial bookings grew a massive 228% Y/Y CC, which reflects
significant further acceleration from the very strong 111% growth rate reported previously and
the 30% Y/Y before that in F4Q. Commercial RPO of $625B also grew an impressive 110% Y/Y
CC, materially above the 51% CC growth rate reported last quarter and the 35% Y/Y growth in the
quarter before that. It's also important to note that roughly 45% of MSFT's commercial RPO is
attributable to OpenAI, thus indicating that the remaining portion of commercial RPO grew by
roughly 28% Y/Y this quarter (with Anthropic providing a boost).
- Capex (including cap leases) of $37.5B was above the Street's $35.9B forecast and our $32.6B
estimate. Interestingly, mgmt noted that roughly two-thirds of its Cloud and AI spend pertains to
short-lived assets (CPUs/GPUs), up from roughly half last quarter. Long-lived assets including
finance leases were $6.7B.
- Windows OEM and device revenue was flat Y/Y on a CC basis (vs. 6% growth previously). And
within that, Windows OEM grew by 5% Y/Y, which was due to strong execution, along with a
continued benefit from Win10 EOS. Mgmt noted that these results were ahead of expectations, as
inventory levels remained elevated with increased purchasing ahead of the recent spike in DRAM
prices.
- Paid Microsoft 365 Commercial seat growth was again 6% Y/Y, and has now surpassed 450M
total seats.
- MSFT stated that Microsoft Fabric has eclipsed $2B in ARR, and that it has 31K customers.
