F2Q26 Review: Progresses in Azure and beyond Azure
•
F2Q26 results largely in line; Azure growth affected by supply constraints.
•
Company achieved solid performance beyond Azure supported by its AI 
investments. 
•
Reiterate BUY and TP of US$630.00 (34x FY27E P/E).
Azure and capital investments: Azure delivered 39% (38% in forex neutral) YoY growth 
in the quarter, slightly above the 37% guidance but below Visible Alpha’s consensus of 
40%. Management guided 37-38% YoY growth for the next quarter, also below 39% 
consensus. Capex of the quarter was US$37.5bn (vs.US$34.9bn last quarter), of which 
2/3 was for CPUs and GPUs (increased from ~50% mix last quarter yet is expected to 
remain at this level for next quarter, per mgmt), and the remaining 1/3 of spending is for 
long-term assets such as datacenters. The company added about 1 gigawatt of total 
capacity this quarter but still faces supply constraints as demand remains strong. 
It’s not just Azure. As management highlighted on the call, its capex spending is for 
multiple purposes: 1) first-party AI usage services such as M365 Copilot and GitHub 
Copilot, 2) internal R&D teams, and 3) Azure business. In the continuing situation of supply 
constraints, Azure cannot fully expand its business to serve all the potential demand on 
the market, which would have helped it deliver over 40% growth for the quarter. 
Management also disclosed incremental achievements in other businesses supported by 
its AI investments. For example, its paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats reached 15mn in the 
quarter, and the number of business customers with over 35k seats tripled YoY. By nature, 
SaaS projects/subscriptions usually have higher margin than pure cloud rental business. 
F2Q26 financial highlights: Total revenue growth was 17% YoY, 1% higher than FactSet 
consensus. Total commercial Remaining Performance Obligation (RPO) was US$625bn 
by quarter end, +230% YoY, with weighted avg duration of 2.5 years. Within that, about 
45% is from the recent multi-year Azure services commitment from OpenAI. The remaining 
55% is from a broad-based customer base and represents a healthy 28% YoY growth. 
Operating margin was 47.1% for the quarter, vs 45.5% in F2Q25. Management anticipates 
FY26 full-year operating margin to be slightly up YoY, which would mark FY26 as the third 
year of margin expansion despite AI investments. 
Financial revisions: We keep our FY26E/ 27E revenue and operating income forecasts 
largely unchanged. FY26 GAAP net income/ EPS now includes a US$4.5bn/ US$0.61 
impact from its OpenAI investment net gain in other income in F1H26. Our FY26E capex 
forecast remains at ~US$150bn.
Reiterate BUY and TP of US$630.00, based on 34x FY27E P/E (prior: 40x FY26E) to 
reflect strong Azure momentum. Risks include: slower AI adoption, lagging 
innovation/competition in AI, weak macro picture, regulatory hurdles, and higher-than-
expected operating expenditure.

Risks
•
Slower-than-expected AI adoption. Slower-than-expected AI adoption by enterprises and 
consumers alike would pose a risk to our revenue and earnings forecasts and likely dampen 
sentiment on Microsoft’s investment value.
•
Innovation and competition risks in AI technology development. Microsoft’s leading position 
in AI is built on its generative-AI capabilities. Any disruption from competitors or innovation from 
other streams of AI technology in this rapidly developing field may weaken Microsoft’s leadership 
in this area, posing a risk to our forecasts for revenue growth.
•
Macro weakness. Macro headwinds weigh on IT spending in both the enterprise and personal 
segments, and Microsoft has exposure to most verticals and geographies. Any downturn in the 
global macroeconomic environment would likely lead to weaker revenue and earnings growth 
for Microsoft.
•
Regulatory risks. Microsoft operates in a complex regulatory environment, subject to various 
laws and regulations across the locations it operates in, regarding AI, data privacy, antitrust, 
intellectual property, taxation and ESG, among others. Microsoft may face legal challenges or 
investigations from regulators across the world, which may cause unexpected disruption to its 
business strategy, revenue and earnings growth.
•
Higher-than-expected operating spending. Ongoing improvement of operational efficiency 
has been one of the key drivers of Microsoft’s earnings growth in recent years. We forecast 
operating margin to continue to improve during our forecast period. Higher-than-expected 
operating expenditure would weigh on Microsoft’s earnings growth outlook and pose a risk to our 
forecasts.
